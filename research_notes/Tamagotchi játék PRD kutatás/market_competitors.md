## 1. Competitor profiles: core loop, death and absence, social features, platform, monetization, scale

### Takeaway
The commercially dominant virtual pets have all moved away from the original Tamagotchi's "neglect = death" design. Outfit7 (My Talking Tom family) and Pou win on download scale through ad-funded, kid-skewed free-to-play care loops with mini-games. Pokémon Sleep and Finch win on revenue per user by tying the pet to a real-life behaviour (sleep, self-care) and charging a subscription. Bandai keeps the classic, consequence-heavy loop alive only in physical toys and console spin-offs. Its mobile/Apple Arcade product (Tamagotchi Adventure Kingdom) is an Animal Crossing-style adventure, not a care sim. That leaves no scaled mobile product that pairs authentic Tamagotchi-style stakes with adult-friendly, ad-light monetization.

### Cited Findings

#### Pou (Zakeh Limited / Paul Salameh) — mobile (Android, iOS; originally also BlackBerry 10), launched 2012
- Made by Lebanese designer Paul Salameh and published under the name Zakeh Limited. Released in 2012 for BlackBerry 10, iOS and Android — [Wikipedia: Pou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pou_(video_game)).
- Downloads conflict across sources:
  - Wikipedia gives an estimate of about 100M downloads across all platforms and notes that Salameh never confirmed download numbers [EST, older] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pou_(video_game)).
  - Fan sources repeat "500M+" downloads [LOW] — [Fandub DB wiki](https://fandubdb.fandom.com/wiki/Pou).
  - Poupedia (fan wiki) says the Google Play listing shows "1,000,000,000+" and that the game reached 1B Google Play downloads in October 2024 [LOW, but consistent with the store-tier display] — [Poupedia](https://poupedia.com/Pou_(game)).
  - No official developer figure was found.
- Core loop: feed, clean, sleep and play (described by low-quality guides). Mini-games are the main coin source, and coins buy food, potions, outfits and room items. Neglect leads to a sad or sick Pou, and potions bought in the "lab" cure sickness. No source documents a death mechanic or any special handling of absence [LOW] — [bitcoinworld Pou guide](https://bitcoinworld.co.in/pou-virtual-pet-guide/); [Fanlore](https://fanlore.org/wiki/Pou).
- Monetization: free with ads and IAP. No reliable revenue figure was found.

Completing real-life self-care goals (journaling, breathing exercises, drinking water) gives the bird energy to go on "adventures", grow and reveal personality — [Paste Magazine](https://www.pastemagazine.com/article/finch-app-mental-health-virtual-pet-self-care); [Her Campus](https://www.hercampus.com/wellness/i-tried-finch-wellness-app-habit-tracker/).

Reviews say skipping a day does not kill the pet, which removes guilt — [HabitBox review 2026](https://habitbox.app/blog/finch-app-review).
- Founders are ex-Quora employees Stephanie Yuan and Thomas "Nino" Nugraha Budi. Finch Care is a Public Benefit Corporation. Public trackers show no institutional funding; one blog reports a small friends-and-family round in late 2021 [EST] — [Paste](https://www.pastemagazine.com/tech/finch/finch-app-mental-health-virtual-pet-self-care); [Review Rounds](https://review-rounds.com/finch-business-model-bootstrapped-self-care-pet-app/).

## 4. AI companion pets (2024–2026): products, user sentiment, controversies

### Takeaway
LLM-powered "companions" grew into a $120M+/year app category in 2025, with revenue per download up sharply. Pet-shaped AI companions (Tolan, Pengu/Friends) and physical AI pets (Casio Moflin, Ropet) are the softest, least controversial edge of it. ## 5. Retention benchmarks (D1 / D7 / D30) for casual and pet games

### Takeaway
GameAnalytics' 2026 benchmark report (2025 data) puts median mobile games at about 22% D1, under 4% D7 and about 0.7–0.8% D30. The top quartile reaches roughly 26–33% D1. Casual-genre figures attributed to AppsFlyer cluster around 28–36% D1, 9–17% D7 and 3–7% D30. Those come from secondary blogs and use per-app averages, which run higher than medians. No pet-genre-specific public benchmark was found.

### Cited Findings
- **GameAnalytics 2026 Mobile & PC Benchmarks (2025 data), classic retention, medians:** D1 about 22%, D7 "just under 4%", D30 about 0.68–0.79% — [GameAnalytics 2026](https://www.gameanalytics.com/reports/2026-mobile-pc-gaming-benchmarks); [InvestGame summary PDF, Jan 2026](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2026-01-20-Mobile_retention_benchmarks_2026.pdf).
- **GameAnalytics 2025 report (2024 data):** top-25% D1 of 26.48–27.69%; D7 median range 3.42–3.94% — [GameAnalytics 2025](https://www.gameanalytics.com/reports/2025-mobile-gaming-benchmarks); [GameDevReports summary](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-gaming-benchmarks).
- **Platform split for the top 25% D1:** about 31–33% on iOS versus 25–27% on Android (GameAnalytics via secondary summary) — [AppAgent](https://appagent.com/blog/mobile-game-retention-benchmarks/).
- **Secondary summaries of the top quartile:** just above 30% D1, 6–7% D7, 1.6–1.8% D30 — [GameGrowthAdvisor, Mar 2026](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-mobile-game-kpis-benchmarks-2026/).
- **Casual genre, AppsFlyer-derived (secondary; AppsFlyer uses per-app averages with the top and bottom 10% trimmed):**
  - Casual/puzzle on Android: 28–32% D1, 9–12% D7, 3.5–5% D30. This is said to come from an AppsFlyer Q3 2022 dataset [LOW] — [GameGrowthAdvisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-mobile-game-retention-strategies-2026/).
  - Other blogs: casual at 30–40% D1, 15–22% D7, 5–10% D30 — [Enable3](https://enable3.io/blog/app-retention-benchmarks-2025). And 30–36% D1, 13–17% D7, 5–7% D30 — [Apsteq](https://apsteq.com/blog/app-retention-benchmarks/) [LOW].
  - Methodology: AppsFlyer averages per app and trims the top and bottom 10% — [summary in Spreadcast](https://spreadcast.app/resources/app-retention-benchmarks).
- **Genre table of unclear attribution:** hyper-casual at 29.31% D1 and 1.38% D30; puzzle at 31.85% D1 and 5.35% D30 [LOW] — [Digital Minds BPO](https://digitalmindsbpo.com/blog/mobile-game-retention-benchmarks/).

### Inferences
- For PRD targets, a reasonable "good" bar for a casual pet game is about 30–35% D1, about 10–12% D7 and about 4–6% D30 on AppsFlyer-style averages. Under GameAnalytics medians, about 22% D1 and about 4% D7 is merely typical.
- Pet games' daily-check-in loop should help D7 and D30 relative to puzzle games: Finch's founders cite attachment as the fix for day-2 churn. This is unproven by public data.

### Gaps
- No Adjust or Liftoff 2025–2026 casual retention figures were retrieved. No virtual-pet- or simulation-specific D1/D7/D30 benchmark was found.
- AppsFlyer's primary benchmark tool could not be opened, so the casual figures rest on secondary blogs.

## 6. Gaps and opportunities: what players and press say is missing or annoying

### Takeaway
Recurring complaints are:
- intrusive ads, especially forced interstitials and unlabeled ads in kid-oriented pet apps;
- over-demanding care loops and nagging notifications;
- guilt and anxiety, which led modern products such as Finch and Bitzee to remove death and punishment entirely;
- shallow or overstated AI and hardware friction in AI pets.

The resulting opportunity for nostalgic adults is a premium or ad-free Tamagotchi-like with real but humane consequences, respectful notifications, depth (genetics, generations, collection) and optional social co-care.

### Cited Findings
- **Ads:**
  - My Talking Tom 2 is "packed full of ads": random video ads, banners and constant purchase prompts — [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/my-talking-tom-2).
  - CARU found unlabeled ads and inappropriate third-party ads in My Talking Tom (2019) — [BBB National Programs](https://bbbprograms.org/archive/caru-s-not-kitten-around-during-review-of-outfit7-s-my-talking-tom-app-recommends-modifications-company-says-it-will-do-so).
  - Dogotchi reviewers complain an ad shows every time they stop playing — [Google Play: Dogotchi](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mawges.dogotchi&hl=en_US). Pet Pals reviewers complain of ads "after every single level" — [Google Play: Pet Pals](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.pet.palpaw&hl=en_GB).
  - RetroMon is praised because ads "rarely pop up" — [App Store: RetroMon](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/retromon-virtual-pet-monster/id895314905).
- **Over-demanding care and stress:** a Pokipet reviewer says pets get sick "more than 4 times a day" even with full hearts, causing stress — [App Store: Pokipet](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pokipet-raise-virtual-pets/id6443760470).
- **Notification nagging:** TouchArcade said Tamagotchi L.i.f.e. (2013) "lets you relive the iconic virtual pet's constant nagging with constant push notifications" and wished alerts were limited to urgent matters — [TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2013/04/05/tamagotchi-l-i-f-e-lets-you-relive-the-iconic-virtual-pets-constant-nagging-with-constant-push-notifications/).
- **Guilt and death:**
  - Wikipedia's virtual pet article links the 1990s decline partly to guilt over pets dying, quoting "who would want to be guilty of killing a pet—even a pretend pet?" Giga Pets were banned in some schools over beeping and children's constant worry — [Wikipedia: Virtual pet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_pet).
  - Finch: pets never die because death is "too anxiety-inducing" — [Finch FAQ](https://befinch.notion.site/Finch-FAQ-474652d0123d4883ac7a0cd6c8f5aa70).
  - Bitzee: love "only goes up, never down" — [iTWire](https://itwire.com/reviews-sp-288/computers-peripherals/the-bitzee-hamster-ball-brings-a-modern-take-on-retro-digital-pet-collecting).
  - Conversely, the ChatGPT Chat Pet reviewer was struck by finding the pet dead after an absence ("harsher than I imagined") — [note.com](https://note.com/omstructa/n/nae76bb1364ad?hl=en).
- **Streak anxiety (adjacent habit apps):** the market is moving toward "forgiving" streaks — [HabitDoom, 2026](https://habitdoom.com/blog/streak-anxiety-habit-trackers). Some apps still use punishment, e.g. Habit-chi's pet gets sad or runs away — [Hunted](https://www.hunted.space/product/habit-chi).
- **Real-pet guilt research (indirect analogy):** owner guilt predicts depression and anxiety in dog and cat owners — [Human-Animal Interactions, DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/8ccd9abe9bc247acabb1036ab792c202).
- **AI and hardware pets:** Moflin complaints cover the whir, battery life, Bluetooth and overstated intelligence — [Coreiten](https://coreiten.com/en/article/casios-429-moflin-ai-pet-cute-concept-frustrating-reality); [AI Metrix](https://aimetrix.org/casio-moflin-ai-pet-review/).
- **Connected Tamagotchi:** Siliconera flagged Tamaverse issues on Uni — [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/tamagotchi-uni-is-surprisingly-good-at-being-a-wearable-virtual-pet/).
- **Privacy:**
  - Exodus found 29 trackers in My Talking Tom 2 — [Exodus](https://reports.exodus-privacy.eu.org/en/reports/523677).
  - A blog questioned Neopets' new apps' privacy policies — [Rebecca in Print](https://rebeccainprint.com/2026/03/28/neopets-new-mobile-apps-raise-questions-about-privacy/).
  - Mozilla reviewed Uni in "Privacy Not Included" — [Mozilla](https://www.mozillafoundation.org/en/privacynotincluded/tamagotchi-uni/).
- **Depth for adults:** fans recommend Paradise as "the deepest raising game Bandai has made" (editorial) — [TamaVault](https://tamavault.com/devices/uni-vs-paradise/). Paradise adds fighting, families and offspring — [Hoodline](https://hoodline.com/2026/10/tamagotchi-floods-times-square-nov-22-for-free-30th-birthday-bash/).
- **Social co-care traction:** Pengu/Friends' co-parenting model is growing (Q4 2025 US weekly downloads 19K→121K) — [Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q4-unified-top-5-virtual-pet-revenue-us-62f83d1bf5dff39390bc09c4).

### Inferences
- **Positioning gap:** "the Tamagotchi you remember, built for your adult life", with:
  - premium or ad-free monetization: a one-time purchase plus cosmetic IAP or a light subscription, never forced interstitials;
  - real but humane stakes: an opt-in "Classic mode" where a pet can die or leave, alongside a default mode where neglect causes sadness, illness or the pet going away on a "trip" rather than permanent loss, plus memorials or legacy/generations that turn loss into story;
  - schedule-aware care, such as sleep/work hours, pause or "pet-sitter" mode and smart notification budgets, to avoid nagging;
  - depth for collectors (genetics, evolutions, generations, dex);
  - optional co-care with a partner or friend (the Pengu signal);
  - widgets, Live Activities and Watch support for glanceable care (the original Tamagotchi's "always with you" quality);
  - possibly a bounded LLM layer for personality and diary, with SB 243-compliant disclosure and minor safeguards.
- Bandai holds the IP and authenticity but has not shipped a mobile care sim; Adventure Kingdom is an adventure game. An independent entrant must compete on design (pixel-retro aesthetic, LCD-feel) without IP. It should expect Bandai to possibly move into mobile around or after the 30th anniversary (2026), which is a risk to monitor.
- Ad-funded incumbents' kid-skew and regulatory baggage (CARU, trackers) make "no ads, no tracking, no dark patterns" a credible differentiator for adults.

### Gaps
- Systematic review mining (e.g. Appbot or AppFollow sentiment for Pou, My Talking Tom 2 and Finch) was not available. The complaint evidence is anecdotal and drawn from individual store reviews and press.
- No survey data on adults' willingness to pay for a premium virtual pet app was found.
- Specific complaints about Tamagotchi Uni's Tamaverse were not retrieved in detail.
