# Original Bandai Tamagotchi (1996/1997) Mechanics and Their Evolution in Later Official Devices and Apps (as of 28 Oct 2026)

How these notes were made: direct page fetches of tamagotchi.fandom.com, bandainamco.co.jp and mimitchi.com were blocked by this session's network allowlist. Every finding below therefore comes from search-engine excerpts of the cited pages, not from reading the full pages. Exact numbers marked "(fan source)" come only from community wikis, forums or fan guides (Tamagotchi Wiki/Fandom, Thaao's Tamas, TamaTalk, Tama-Palace, TamaVault). Bandai has never published these timer numbers in official documentation that I could find. To verify, allow tamagotchi.fandom.com, bandainamco.co.jp and mimitchi.com in the network settings and re-check.

## 1. Original P1/P2 (1996-1997) core care stats, meters and timings

### Takeaway
The original P1 has a small set of states. Hunger runs 0-4 hearts and Happy runs 0-4 hearts. Discipline is shown as 0-100% in 25% steps. The device also tracks weight, age, up to 4 poops on screen, a sickness flag (skull icon) and a lights on/off state. Each call ignored for about 15 minutes counts as a "care mistake". Hearts drain on the order of one heart every 3-7 minutes, but this number comes only from fan sources and the sources conflict.

### Cited Findings
**Meters**
- Hunger and Happy each run from 0 to 4 hearts. With all 4 hunger hearts filled, the pet is "full". When either meter reaches 0, the pet calls for attention (beeps and the attention icon lights up). — [Tamagotchi Wiki: Tamagotchi (1996 Pet)](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Tamagotchi_(1996_Pet)); [Thaao's Tamas P1 Care Guide](https://thaao.net/tama/p1/) (fan sources)
- Heart drain rate (fan sources, conflicting):
  - The Tamagotchi Wiki says the loss rate on the original releases "caps at 1 Hungry heart every 6 minutes, and 1 Happy heart every 7 minutes". — [Tamagotchi Wiki: Tamagotchi (1996 Pet)](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Tamagotchi_(1996_Pet))
  - A Tama-Palace comparison of original vs replica says the original Babytchi loses 1 Hungry heart every 3 minutes and 1 Happy heart every 4 minutes. — [Tama-Palace: Original vs Replica, Baby Stage](https://tamapalace.tumblr.com/post/180272055671/tamagotchi-original-vs-replica-baby-stage)
  - A TamaTalk forum user recalls that on the P1/P2, hearts drain "faster and faster until it empties every few seconds" late in life, which makes the pet impossible to care for. This is an anecdotal recollection. — [TamaTalk: Gen 1 care questions](https://www.tamatalk.com/threads/gen-1-tamagotchi-care-questions.198041/)
- Discipline: each correct scold raises the bar by 25%, so there are 4 segments from 0 to 100%. On the 1996-97 originals, the pet "will misbehave exactly enough times to fill its Discipline level to 100%". — [Thaao's Tamas P1 Care Guide](https://thaao.net/tama/p1/) (fan source)
- Misbehaviour ("attention call" with no real need): the pet calls even though its meters are not empty, and the correct response is to scold it. On the P1, Thaao says skipping a scold does not add a care mistake, but it does lower discipline, which affects evolution. — [Thaao's Tamas P1 Care Guide](https://thaao.net/tama/p1/). A Tamagotchi Wiki "Care" excerpt instead says you get a "disciplinary mistake" if you do not discipline, even when the meter is full. That excerpt also says discipline "starts at 50%" and that discipline calls happen only when the pet has at least one heart of each type. These statements are probably generic or about later models, so they conflict with the P1-specific Thaao guide. — [Tamagotchi Wiki: Care](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Care)
- The status screens show age, weight, discipline, hunger and happy. — [Tamagotchi Wiki: Care](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Care)

**Calls and care mistakes**
- Each call has one correct response: hunger call → meal; happy call → snack or game; sleep call → turn off the light; misbehaviour call → scold. — [Thaao's Tamas P1 Care Guide](https://thaao.net/tama/p1/)
- Care mistake rule: a care mistake is logged when the Hunger or Happy meter is at 0 and is not fixed within 15 minutes. — [Tamagotchi Wiki: Care](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Care); [Thaao's Tamas P1 Care Guide](https://thaao.net/tama/p1/); repeated on [TamaTalk P1/P2 Evolution Guide](https://www.tamatalk.com/threads/tamagotchi-p1-p2-evolution-guide.200023/) (fan sources; at least two agree)
- Lights: not turning the light off within 15 minutes of the pet falling asleep is a care mistake. — [Thaao's Tamas P1 Care Guide](https://thaao.net/tama/p1/)
- Later models (the "P's" era) use different windows: 15 minutes for empty hunger or happy, and 60 minutes for sickness or sulking. This does not describe the original. — [TamaTalk P1/P2 Evolution Guide](https://www.tamatalk.com/threads/tamagotchi-p1-p2-evolution-guide.200023/); [Tamagotchi Wiki: Tamagotchi P's character list](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Tamagotchi_P's/Character_list)
- The Tamagotchi Wiki splits care mistakes into "physical" (missed empty-hunger call, missed scold call, untreated sickness) and "mental" (missed empty-happy call or praise call). The wiki says this applies across releases, but the split mainly matters on later models. — [Tamagotchi Wiki: Care](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Care)

**Poop, sickness and medicine**
- Poop appears on the right side of the screen, and up to 4 can pile up. The pet does NOT call when it poops. The flush/duck icon clears all of them at once. Leaving poop on screen causes sickness. — [Thaao's Tamas P1 Care Guide](https://thaao.net/tama/p1/)
- Sickness: a skull icon appears next to the pet, and the pet does NOT call. The fix is to press the medicine icon repeatedly until the pet recovers. Illness happens once per evolution stage, plus whenever poop is left too long. A slow response to sickness causes no care mistake, but a pet left sick too long dies. — [Thaao's Tamas P1 Care Guide](https://thaao.net/tama/p1/); [Tamagotchi Wiki: Sickness](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Sickness)
- Babytchi reportedly falls ill about 33 minutes after hatching and needs 2 doses of medicine. This comes from a single fan source. — [Thaao's Tamas P1 Care Guide](https://thaao.net/tama/p1/)
- 2017/2018 re-releases: the medicine icon is a syringe on all regional versions. — [TamaVault Gen 2 Re-Release Guide](https://tamavault.com/devices/original-gen2/)

**Snacks and weight**
- Overfeeding snacks did NOT cause death on the 1990s originals (forum claim). On the 2017+ re-releases, over-snacking can kill a child or teen. — [TamaTalk: Gen 1 care questions](https://www.tamatalk.com/threads/gen-1-tamagotchi-care-questions.198041/); [TamaVault Gen 1 Re-Release Guide](https://tamavault.com/devices/original-gen1/)

**Sleep and age**
- Bedtimes differ by character. Examples: Marutchi (child) sleeps 8 PM-9 AM; Mametchi (adult) sleeps 10 PM-9 AM. — [Thaao's Tamas P1 Care Guide](https://thaao.net/tama/p1/)
- Age goes up 1 "year" every time the pet wakes from sleep, including the baby's short nap. — [Tamagotchi Wiki: Tamagotchi (1996 Pet)](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Tamagotchi_(1996_Pet))
- The baby stage lasts exactly 65 minutes: 60 minutes awake, then a 5-minute nap. — [Tamagotchi Wiki: Baby stage](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Baby_stage); [Tama-Palace: Original vs Replica](https://tamapalace.tumblr.com/post/180272055671/tamagotchi-original-vs-replica-baby-stage)

**P1 vs P2 differences**
- The P2 was released shortly after the P1. It has new characters, a new game, and new food and snacks, but "very similar" programming. Its cast differs by region: Japanese series 1 has Pochitchi and Sekitoritchi, Japanese series 2 has Nyatchi and Charitchi, and the English version has Pochitchi and Zatchi. Some P2 releases use updated programming in which care mistakes affect evolution more. — [Thaao's Tamas P2 Character Guide](https://thaao.net/tama/p2/?p=chara); [TamaTalk: A guide to the P2](https://www.tamatalk.com/threads/a-guide-to-the-tamagotchi-p2.177796/)
- The Gen 1 and Gen 2 re-releases share no characters. They have different minigames ("Left or Right" vs "Higher or Lower") and different food art (bread and candy vs hamburger and cake). — [TamaVault Gen 2 Re-Release Guide](https://tamavault.com/devices/original-gen2/)

### Inferences
- A developer-ready baseline taken from the fan numbers above. Use it as a design starting point, not as confirmed original firmware:
  - Hunger: 4 hearts. Happy: 4 hearts.
  - Heart decay: about 1 heart per 3-4 minutes in the baby stage, about 1 per 6-7 minutes later.
  - Care-mistake window: 15 minutes for empty hunger, empty happy, or lights left on.
  - Discipline: 4 steps of 25%.
  - Poop: max 4 on screen; uncleaned poop causes sickness.
  - Sickness: a fixed number of medicine doses (2 for the baby).
  - Age: +1 per wake-up.
  - Baby stage: 65 minutes.
- Sickness and poop are "silent" (no call), while hunger, happy, sleep and misbehaviour make the pet call. The player has to check the screen, and this is a deliberate part of the design.

### Gaps
- No official Bandai specification of heart-decay rates was found. The fan sources disagree on whether decay speeds up or slows down with age. The Wiki excerpt calls 6 and 7 minutes a "cap", which conflicts with faster baby rates and with forum reports of decay accelerating in old age.
- Weight effects per action (for example, how many weight units a meal, snack or game changes) did not appear in any search result I could access. Commonly repeated fan values were not verified, so they are left out.
- Bedtimes for all P1 characters (teen and other adult schedules) beyond the Marutchi and Mametchi examples were not retrieved.
- The minigame's exact mechanics and its effect on happy hearts and weight were not retrieved.

## 2. Life cycle, evolution chart and the role of care mistakes and discipline

### Takeaway
The P1 chain is egg → Babytchi (baby, about 65 minutes) → Marutchi (child) → Tamatchi or Kuchitamatchi (teen) → one of several adults → a possible secret/special character. Care mistakes in the child stage choose the teen. Teen-stage care mistakes plus discipline choose the adult. Fan guides partly disagree on the exact thresholds.

### Cited Findings
- Babytchi always becomes Marutchi; nothing done during the baby stage affects the outcome. — [TamaTalk P1/P2 Evolution Guide](https://www.tamatalk.com/threads/tamagotchi-p1-p2-evolution-guide.200023/)
- Care mistakes in the Marutchi stage decide the teen: fewer mistakes → Tamatchi, more mistakes → Kuchitamatchi. — [TamaTalk P1/P2 Evolution Guide](https://www.tamatalk.com/threads/tamagotchi-p1-p2-evolution-guide.200023/)
- Teen discipline sets a hidden "version": 75% or higher → "good" version; 0-50% → "bad" version. — [TamaTalk P1/P2 Evolution Guide](https://www.tamatalk.com/threads/tamagotchi-p1-p2-evolution-guide.200023/)
- From Tamatchi: 0-2 care mistakes → a "good" adult; 3 or more → a "bad" adult. Kuchitamatchi always becomes one of the three lower adults, whatever its care mistakes. Only teen-stage care mistakes count toward the adult. — [TamaTalk P1/P2 Evolution Guide](https://www.tamatalk.com/threads/tamagotchi-p1-p2-evolution-guide.200023/)
- Mametchi (the best adult): Tamatchi with 0-2 care mistakes and 100% discipline. Kuchipatchi: Tamatchi with 3 or more care mistakes and 100% discipline. — [TamaTalk P1/P2 Evolution Guide](https://www.tamatalk.com/threads/tamagotchi-p1-p2-evolution-guide.200023/); [Tamagotchi Wiki: 1996 Pet character list](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Tamagotchi_(1996_Pet)/Character_list)
- Conflicting fan view: a Tama Zone post maps Mametchi to good care, Ginjirotchi to average care and Maskutchi to poor care, and argues Tamatchi can reach any of the six adults. — [Tama Zone: Tamagotchi Original Growth Chart](https://www.tapatalk.com/groups/tamazone/tamagotchi-original-growth-chart-created-for-tz-t26519.html)
- The 20th-anniversary replicas (2017-2018) "use different programming", so growth rules taken from a replica may not match the 1996-97 hardware. — [TamaTalk: Tamagotchi Original Full Guide](https://www.tamatalk.com/threads/tamagotchi-original-full-guide.201737/)
- Fan character-list lifespans, from search excerpts of the fan character lists (not verified against a second source):
  - Mametchi: 15-16 days.
  - Tarakotchi (worst-care adult, "the neediest"): 3-4 days.
  - Bill (P1 special character, international versions only): 15-16 days.
  - Zuccitchi: 15-16 days.
  - The excerpt says several adults, including Maskutchi and the special characters, share the 15-16 day range.
  - — [Tamagotchi Wiki: 1996 Pet character list](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Tamagotchi_(1996_Pet)/Character_list); [Thaao's Tamas P1 Character Guide](http://thaao.net/tama/p1/?p=chara); [TamaTalk: Average life span of P2](https://www.tamatalk.com/threads/average-life-span-of-tamagotchi-p2.172339/)
- Paradise (2025) still uses care mistakes as the main driver of which adult a pet becomes. Its tiers run from perfect care to heavy neglect (6 or more mistakes). — [TamaVault: Paradise Care Mistakes](https://tamavault.com/paradise/care-mistakes/) (fan source)

### Inferences
- The design pattern that works: two hidden variables, care mistakes (a count) and discipline (a percentage), are sampled at fixed stage boundaries, and a small lookup table maps them to branches. Care quality therefore has visible, delayed consequences, which gives replay value.
- Lifespan and evolution are coupled: better-care adults live longer, and the worst-care adults (such as Tarakotchi) live only 3-4 days. A clone game can use the adult character to set the maximum lifespan.

### Gaps
- Exact child-stage care-mistake thresholds (for example, how many mistakes push Marutchi to Kuchitamatchi) and stage durations in hours (child and teen) were not in the accessible excerpts. A TamaTalk thread titled "Vintage (P1/P2) Growth Times?" exists ([link](https://www.tamatalk.com/threads/vintage-p1-p2-growth-times.198793/)), but I could not read it.
- The P1 secret character's trigger conditions were not confirmed. Bill is the international name in the character-list excerpt; the Japanese counterpart's name and the trigger conditions were not retrieved.
- The Zuccitchi lifespan in the excerpt (15-16 days) seems high for a poor-care adult and could be a summarisation error. Check it against the full Wiki character list.

## 3. Death: causes, speed, lifespans and the death screen (Japan vs international)

### Takeaway
On the 1996-97 originals, pets died of old age, prolonged sickness, or neglect. A full reset then started a new egg. No official "hours to death" figure exists. Fan lifespans run from about 3-4 days (worst adults) to 15-16 days (best adults), with claims of up to 25-28 days. Japanese units showed a ghost beside a gravestone; English P1 units showed an angel among stars, and the English P2 showed a UFO. Later Bandai devices softened death in stages: running away (iD), no death at all (4U), and "moving away" (Pix). Newer devices (Uni, Paradise) still have real death, but add rescue mechanics.

### Cited Findings
**Death on the originals**
- Causes: old age, with a maximum lifespan that varies by character; sickness left untreated too long; and neglect. — [Tamagotchi Wiki: Tamagotchi (1996 Pet)](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Tamagotchi_(1996_Pet)); [Thaao's Tamas P1 Care Guide](https://thaao.net/tama/p1/)
- Typical lifespan claims (fan sources, conflicting):
  - about 12 "years"/days typical, or up to 25 "years" (24 days) — [90sToys: How long do Tamagotchi live](https://www.90stoys.com/electronic-toys/how-long-do-tamagotchi-live/)
  - 7-12 days, per a forum post — [TamaTalk: Gen 1 care questions](https://www.tamatalk.com/threads/gen-1-tamagotchi-care-questions.198041/)
  - a claimed maximum of 28 days — [TamaTalk: Can tamagotchis die of old age?](https://www.tamatalk.com/threads/can-tamagotchis-die-of-old-age.184236/)
- Neglect speed: a general (not P1-specific) guide says "if you don't feed your Tamagotchi for 12 hours, it might die". — [TamagotchiPet: Dead Tamagotchi](https://tamagotchipet.com/dead-tamagotchi/). TechCrunch describes the original rule informally: ignoring the pet "for a few days" killed it. — [TechCrunch, 15 Mar 2018](https://techcrunch.com/2018/03/15/my-tamagotchi-forever-brings-the-90s-to-your-smartphone)
- Can babies die? Sources conflict: one says not as a baby, only from the child stage; another says babies can die on P1/P2. — [90sToys](https://www.90stoys.com/electronic-toys/how-long-do-tamagotchi-live/); [TamaTalk: Gen 1 care questions](https://www.tamatalk.com/threads/gen-1-tamagotchi-care-questions.198041/)
- After death, the only way to continue is a reset, which wipes the unit and starts a new egg. — [Retro Radical: Tamagotchi 90s](https://retroradical.com/tamagotchi-90s-virtual-pet-craze/)

**Death screens**
- Japan: a ghost (Obaketchi) floating beside a gravestone. — [Tamagotchi Wiki: Tamagotchi (1996 Pet)](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Tamagotchi_(1996_Pet)); [Tamagotchi Wiki: vintage franchise](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Tamagotchi_(vintage_franchise))
- English/international P1: an angel flying among stars. English P2: a UFO. — [Thaao's Tamas P2 Care Guide](https://thaao.net/tama/p2/); [Tamagotchi Wiki: Tamagotchi (1996 Pet)](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Tamagotchi_(1996_Pet))
- Tamagotchi Angel (1997 spin-off): the Japanese version shows Obaketchi beside a grave; the international version replaced the tombstone with a UFO. — [Buy-Tamagotchis: Tamagotchi Angel guide](https://buy-tamagotchis.com/tamagotchi-angel-guide/)
- Commentary describes the Western angel as the "more religiously universal" option. — [Retro Radical](https://retroradical.com/tamagotchi-90s-virtual-pet-craze/)

**Softened death in later devices**
- Tamagotchi iD (Japan, colour era): neglected adults could run away instead of dying, leaving a crying letter that asks for better care. Tamagotchi 4U removed death entirely and kept only the run-away sequence. — [TamagotchiPet: Dead Tamagotchi](https://tamagotchipet.com/dead-tamagotchi/); [Tamagotchi Wiki: Death](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Death)
- Tamagotchi Pix (2021): instead of dying, the pet looks unwell, a small suitcase appears, the character waves goodbye and walks off screen ("moves away"). After a long life this works as a "good ending". — [TamagotchiPet: Dead Tamagotchi](https://tamagotchipet.com/dead-tamagotchi/)
- Grim Gotchi mechanic (introduced with the Tamagotchi m!x):
  - If a pet gets sick because of a care mistake, a Grim Gotchi may appear instead, and more appear over time.
  - At 4 Grim Gotchis, the pet dies. The number 4 is chosen because "shi" (four) sounds like the Japanese word for death (死).
  - An ambulance can rescue the pet; the excerpt describes this as available while 3 or fewer Grim Gotchis are present.
  - The Uni and Smart death screen is Obaketchi crying on a plain black background.
  - Monster Carnival exclusive characters on the Uni have a special sequence in which Grim Gotchis chase away the Chestnut Angels.
  - — [Tamagotchi Wiki: Grim Gotchi](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Grim_Gotchi); [Tamagotchi Wiki: Death](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Death); [Tamagotchi Wiki: Tamagotchi Hospital](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Tamagotchi_Hospital)
- Tamagotchi Paradise (2025):
  - A pet dies if it gets sick 5 times within one growth stage, or if the hunger meter stays empty too long.
  - After death it becomes a "Tama Star" that records the age at death and the cause, such as "Neglect" or "Tama Sick".
  - Deaths before age 30 use the standard sequence, in which Chestnut Angels escort the pet away.
  - Deaths at age 30 or older get a "peaceful death" and a special Tama Star. That star automatically saves a later pet from death once, by chasing away the Grim Gotchis.
  - — [Tamagotchi Wiki: Tamagotchi Paradise](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Tamagotchi_Paradise); [Tamagotchi Wiki: Paradise character list](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Tamagotchi_Paradise/Character_list)

### Inferences
- Bandai's arc runs from permanent, unpausable death (1996), to run-away and no-death variants (mid-2000s and 2010s colour era), to death with rescue and legacy mechanics (Uni and Paradise in 2023-2025: ambulance, Tama Star). This suggests a modern design can keep the stakes but should give players a visible rescue path and a positive legacy outcome.
- Japanese vs international death screens are a clear precedent for regional or cultural localisation of death imagery (ghost and grave vs angel or UFO).

### Gaps
- No official Bandai figure exists for how fast a fully neglected P1 dies, for example hours at 0 hearts before death. Only the general "12 hours" claim and the "few days" claim were found.
- The exact number of care mistakes, or the hours sick, that kill a P1 pet could not be verified.
- Whether My Tamagotchi Forever (2018) or Tamagotchi Adventure Kingdom (2024) allow pet death could not be confirmed.

## 4. Real-time tick, sleep, pause and battery/reset behaviour

### Takeaway
The originals ran in real time against an owner-set clock, with no real pause. The only workaround was sitting on the clock-setting screen, which stopped time. The lack of a pause is cited as a reason schools banned the toy. Later models added a pause, which was later replaced by paid or limited "babysitter"/hotel services. Removing the batteries on a 1990s unit lost the pet; re-releases reportedly offer a "download" restore.

### Cited Findings
- The original 1997 Tamagotchi could only be "paused" by entering the clock-setting screen, which stops the in-game clock. Later models added a dedicated pause, which was later replaced by a paid babysitting feature. — [Wikipedia: Tamagotchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamagotchi)
- Clock-screen trick: press B to show the clock, then hold A+C until the hour blinks. Time stops, so a pet paused for 8 hours evolves 8 hours later. The screen keeps drawing battery while paused. — [ScienceDesk: How to pause a Tamagotchi](https://sciencedesk.blog/how-to-pause-tamagotchi) (low-authority blog); [Tama-Palace: How to pause a Tamagotchi Original](https://tamapalace.tumblr.com/post/771121504557596672/how-to-pause-a-tamagotchi-original)
- Tama-Palace says earlier American releases "have always included a pause feature", but the modern Tamagotchi Original re-release does not, hence the clock workaround. — [Tama-Palace](https://tamapalace.tumblr.com/post/771121504557596672/how-to-pause-a-tamagotchi-original). This conflicts with the Wikipedia claim that the original 1997 unit had no pause, so it may refer to specific 1997 English variants. Unresolved.
- The Museum of Play says 1997 school bans (China, Hong Kong, Thailand, US) were partly because a Tamagotchi's life cycle could not be paused. — [The Strong National Museum of Play](https://www.museumofplay.org/games/tamagotchi/)
- Sleep runs on per-character bedtimes, for example Marutchi 8 PM-9 AM and Mametchi 10 PM-9 AM. Age increments on waking. — [Thaao's Tamas P1 Care Guide](https://thaao.net/tama/p1/); [Tamagotchi Wiki: 1996 Pet](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Tamagotchi_(1996_Pet))
- Batteries and reset:
  - The 1990s originals used two LR44 cells; the re-releases use one CR2032. — [TamaVault: Troubleshooting](https://tamavault.com/troubleshooting/); [TamaVault Gen 1 Re-Release Guide](https://tamavault.com/devices/original-gen1/)
  - The 1997 units had no download/restore, so removing the batteries meant starting over. — [Tama Zone: Does your pet survive battery replacement?](https://www.tapatalk.com/groups/tamazone/does-your-pet-survive-battery-replacement-t26463.html); [TamaTalk: if the batteries run out](https://www.tamatalk.com/threads/if-the-batteries-run-out-will-i-lose-my-tamagotchi.170295/)
  - The Gen 1 and Gen 2 re-releases offer a "Download" option after a reset that restores the pet alive at the time of battery death or reset. — [TamaVault: Troubleshooting](https://tamavault.com/troubleshooting/)
  - The re-releases also add a low-battery warning. — [TamaVault Gen 2 Re-Release Guide](https://tamavault.com/devices/original-gen2/)
- Pause and sitter features on later models:
  - Tamagotchi Connection: one source says the pause function arrived on the international version. — [Tamagotchi Wiki: Connection V1](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Tamagotchi_Connection_Version_1) (single source)
  - Connection-era "Tama Hotel": 10 Gotchi Points (GP) per hour. — [TamaTalk: Pausing/babysitting](https://www.tamatalk.com/threads/pausing-babysitting.197592/)
  - Tamagotchi Pix: nanny is free, 7 AM-7 PM. — [ScienceDesk](https://sciencedesk.blog/how-to-pause-tamagotchi)
  - Tamagotchi Uni: sitter hired through the in-game phone for 100 GP, 7 AM-6 PM. — [ScienceDesk](https://sciencedesk.blog/how-to-pause-tamagotchi)
  - Tamagotchi On: pausing is possible (how-to guide exists). — [Tama-Palace: How to pause Tamagotchi On](https://www.tumblr.com/tamapalace/187332957308/how-to-pause-tamagotchi-on)

### Inferences
- Paused progress from the clock trick delays evolution one-for-one in real time. A clone can model pause as a full freeze of the tick.
- The design arc runs from no pause (1996), to a free pause (mid-2000s), to a time-boxed daytime babysitter that sometimes costs in-game currency (Pix and Uni). The babysitter covers school hours but does not allow indefinite freezing.

### Gaps
- Whether particular 1997 English P1/P2 SKUs had a built-in pause could not be resolved; the sources conflict.
- Whether the Paradise (2025) has a babysitter or pause could not be confirmed.
- Exact babysitter behaviour (which meters it maintains, whether care mistakes are prevented) was not retrieved for the Pix and Uni.

## 5. Generations: what happens after death, and family, marriage and generations from the Connection (2004) onwards

### Takeaway
The 1996-97 units had no lineage: death meant a reset and a fresh egg. The Tamagotchi Connection (Tamagotchi Plus in Japan, 2004) introduced infrared "Tamacom" play, marriage and offspring. This created a generation counter that continues the family instead of ending at death. Later devices, up to the Paradise in 2025, keep breeding as a way to continue a line.

### Cited Findings
- Original: on death, the player resets the device to get a new egg; no data carries over. — [Retro Radical](https://retroradical.com/tamagotchi-90s-virtual-pet-craze/)
- The Tamagotchi Connection launched in Japan in March 2004 as "Tamagotchi Plus". North American launch sources disagree: 15 August 2004 vs 10 August 2004 (a launch-event date). — [Wikipedia: Tamagotchi Connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamagotchi_Connection); [Tamagotchi Wiki: Connection V1](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Tamagotchi_Connection_Version_1)
- It was the first release with "Tamacom" infrared, which links two pets to play games, swap presents, marry and have offspring. — [Tamagotchi Wiki: Tamacom](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Tamacom); [Wikipedia: Tamagotchi Connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamagotchi_Connection)
- Two adult pets of different genders that connect often enough may fall in love and have offspring. Two babies of the same gender are produced, and each parent device gets one. A matchmaker character can pair a pet with a computer-controlled partner when no human partner is available. — [Wikipedia: Tamagotchi Connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamagotchi_Connection); [Tamagotchi Wiki: Connection V1](https://tamagotchi.fandom.com/wiki/Tamagotchi_Connection_Version_1)

### Inferences
- From 2004 on, the main "end of life" loop is reproduction rather than death. The parent leaves and the child continues, keeping a generation count. This gives a natural, non-morbid way to roll over a save in a modern design.

### Gaps
- The friendship-level thresholds and number of connections needed for marriage on the Connection were not retrieved.
- How Tamagotchi On/Meets (2018/2019) handles marriage across devices (including the gene and appearance mixing it is known for) could not be confirmed in the accessible excerpts.

## 6. Later official versions and apps: what changed (softening of death, pause/babysitter, minigames, connectivity)

### Takeaway
Later Bandai products kept the core loop: feed, play, clean, cure, sleep, discipline, evolve. Each generation added a layer:
- IR social play and breeding (Connection, 2004).
- Cross-device gene mixing and an app (Meets/On, 2018/2019).
- Camera, touch controls and a gentle "moves away" ending (Pix, 2021).
- Wi-Fi with online Tamaverse events, a pedometer and paid sitters (Uni, 2023).
- A zoomable "cell to cosmos" dial with species and fields, and death plus a Tama Star legacy (Paradise, 2025; new variants in 2026).

Official apps have mostly been free-to-play or subscription titles.

## 7. Commercial facts, the 1997 craze, school bans and cultural impact

### Takeaway
Bandai's official cumulative figure is 100 million units shipped worldwide as of end of July 2025, announced 28 August 2025 at Tokyo Toy Show; 51% of shipments were overseas. The 30th anniversary is 23 November 2026 in Japan. The 1997 craze produced sell-outs, school bans in several countries and a 1997 Ig Nobel Prize. It also gave its name to the "Tamagotchi effect" (emotional attachment to machines). Early-year sales figures conflict across secondary sources.

### Cited Findings
**Official figures (dated)**
- "As of the end of July 2025, Tamagotchi had shipped a total of 100 million units worldwide" (Bandai Namco investor newsletter). The 30th anniversary falls in November 2026; overseas markets count 2027 as their 30th year because they launched a year later. — [Bandai Namco IR Newsletter vol. 82 special feature](https://www.bandainamco.co.jp/en/ir/library/newsletter82_special.html)
- The milestone was revealed on 28 August 2025 at the opening ceremony of the Bandai / BANDAI SPIRITS booth at Tokyo Toy Show 2025, by Taro Tsuji, Bandai's "Chief Tamagotchi Officer". Overseas shipments are 51% of the series total. — [Oricon US](https://us.oricon-group.com/news/5941/); [Toy People](https://www.toy-people.com/en/?p=103089)
- Note on wording: Bandai says "shipped"; many outlets say "sold". — [NSS Mag](https://www.nssmag.com/en/lifestyle/38204/tamagotchi-y2k-nostalgia-100-millions-sales); [Quasa](https://quasa.io/media/the-egg-that-hatched-living-robots-tamagotchi-at-30)

### Inferences
- 100 million is a cumulative shipment count, not active users. One commentary explicitly warns against reading it as community size. — [Quasa](https://quasa.io/media/the-egg-that-hatched-living-robots-tamagotchi-at-30)
- Shipments went from roughly 40 million by early 1998 (secondary sources) to 100 million by mid-2025. The second half of sales therefore came over about 27 years of revivals (Connection 2004, colour era, Uni 2023, Paradise 2025), which shows the brand has staying power.

- Year-by-year Bandai shipment figures between 1998 and 2025 (for example the 2017 and 2019 cumulative totals often quoted) were not retrieved from an official source.
